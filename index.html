No Strangers in the Craft

Open the PDF

Good evening, brethren.

Last meeting, WM Swain put a question in front of this district, and I have not been able to put it down. In line with the Grand Master's vision, reconnect and recommit, he asked what stands between our lodges and real commitment. What stands between us and real connection. Tonight I am not bringing you a history lesson. I want to talk about us, right here, right now, and I want to take that theme deeper than we usually take it.

When we hear reconnect, most of us think about getting brothers back in the room. When we hear recommit, we think about getting brothers active again. That is true, but it is the surface. Here is the theme I want to lay in front of this district tonight: there should be no strangers in the Craft.

Let me name what stands between us, because we all know it and we might as well say it out loud. Time. Work. Family. And it does not fall the same for every brother in this room. For some of us, lodge night opens the week. For some of us, it closes the week. Some of us walk in midweek just trying to get over the hump. But wherever your night falls, life is already there, and a man gives his lodge whatever that day has left in him.

Then there is the barrier nobody says out loud, and I learned that one the hard way. At the very first leadership meeting, the Grand Master asked the Worshipful Masters one question: do you know a Worshipful Master in another lodge? The calling was completely random, and I had the pleasure, and I use that word loosely, of being called on first. Brethren, I could not answer him. I was sitting there as a Worshipful Master, and I did not know a Worshipful Master in another lodge well enough to answer his question.

That moment told on all of us. We were leading lodges side by side in the same district and the same jurisdiction, and we were strangers.

I do not believe that question was an accident. The Grand Master already had a name for his theme, reconnect and recommit, and that question was him driving it home in the first few words he had with us, kicking off a new Masonic year under new administrations. But hear me on what that question really exposed. It was not just that we did not know each other's names. Knowing a name is only the beginning. If I do not know you, I cannot count on you. And if I cannot count on you, then when the weight comes, every lodge carries its own load, behind its own doors, until carrying it alone starts to feel normal.

So when I say no strangers in the Craft, I do not mean we all learn each other's names. I mean no brother should be carrying a burden nobody knows about. No brother should be sitting on a gift nobody uses. No brother should be absent and not missed. And no brother should be left uncalled when there is weight to carry. Reconnect is deeper than being recognized. Reconnect is trust rebuilt until a man can be counted on again, and until he knows he can count on us. Recommit is the vow underneath it: I will make myself a man who can be counted on. Findable. Answerable. A man whose word holds when weight is placed on it.

A stranger can sit in the room, brethren. A brother changes what the room can carry.

And look at what happens when this district decides to move together. Next month, the lodges carol together, in the street, where the community can see us and hear us. In a few weeks, Delaware brothers get in cars and travel to Maryland's Grand Session, not because we have to, but because that is what brothers do. And right now there is a Community Day being built for 2027 that no single lodge in this room could carry alone, and together we are carrying it. Those are not just events. That is what it looks like when men stop being strangers and start being men who can count on each other.

Here is what I have learned sitting in the East this year: commitment is not measured by what a man does for his own lodge. Anybody can show up for his own. Commitment is measured by what a man shows up for when his name is not on it, when his lodge is not hosting it, when nobody would notice if he stayed home. That is the commitment that builds a district. That is the commitment that turns multiple lodges into one force.

So here is my ask tonight, and I am asking myself first. It has two parts, and they belong together. First, know a brother: before this year ends, learn one brother outside your own lodge beyond his title. Learn what he carries, what he is good at, what he needs. Second, be a man he can count on: pick one thing this district does together, and be there for it, all the way there, and do not come alone. Bring one brother with you, the one who drifted, the one who needs a reason to come back. That phone call is reconnect. That ride together is recommit.

Brethren, the light in this district is not low because we lack good men. This room is full of good men. The light gets brighter every single time one of us decides that what happens to this district happens to him personally, and that no brother in this district will remain a stranger to him.

So let us decide it tonight.

No strangers in the Craft.

Know your brother. Be counted on. Carry it together.

Thank you.
